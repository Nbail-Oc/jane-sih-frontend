# Case Studies of Deanonymization   
- **Silk Road (Ross Ulbricht, 2013):** Investigators found a flaw in Silk Road’s login page. The site used an anti-abuse CAPTCHA that fetched content from the open Internet. Monitoring that request revealed the actual server’s IP (non-Tor), allowing police to access Silk Road directly. This configuration error exposed Ulbricht’s hidden service.  
- **AlphaBay (Alexandre Cazes, 2017):** AlphaBay’s admin made key OPSEC mistakes. He reused his personal Hotmail address in a welcome email to vendors, which tied back to his real identity. When arrested, Cazes’s laptop was unlocked and logged into AlphaBay’s admin panel; police recovered passwords and other links to him. These oversights (email reuse and an unencrypted admin machine) led to his deanonymization.  
- **Hansa Market (2017, Operation Bayonet):** Rather than a software flaw, Hansa was taken over by Dutch police who ran it as a honeypot. While operating Hansa covertly, investigators modified its code to log users’ data (emails, PGP keys, messages) and collected real IP addresses of vendors and buyers. Migrating AlphaBay users to this police-run site exposed thousands of identities. (No simple “mistake” by criminals – law enforcement engineered the trap.)  
- **Wall Street Market (2019):** In this German-run marketplace, admins themselves pulled an exit-scam, stealing ~$11M from user escrow. Immediately after disappearing, international police closed in. The DOJC complaint notes that the admins “chose to steal funds” then vanished, which prompted their identification and arrest. In other words, their criminal exit-scam betrayed their anonymity.  

# Relevant Academic Papers  
- **Kwon *et al.*, “Circuit Fingerprinting Attacks: Passive Deanonymization of Tor Hidden Services”, USENIX Sec’15** – Introduces passive traffic-analysis attacks that distinguish Tor hidden-service circuits, achieving high true-positive deanonymization rates for clients and servers.  
- **Panchenko *et al.*, “Analysis of Fingerprinting Techniques for Tor Hidden Services”, RAID 2017** – Proposes a two-phase website-fingerprinting attack on onion services (detection then identification) without malicious Tor nodes. They evaluate scalability on large hidden-service datasets.  
- **Ho & Ng, “Application of Stylometry to DarkWeb Forum User Identification” (ICICS 2016)** – Explores writing-style (lexical/syntactic) features to link multiple user accounts across dark-web forums. They show stylometry can help investigators associate aliases on underground forums.  
- **Wang *et al.*, “You Are Your Photographs: Detecting Multiple Identities of Vendors in the Darknet Marketplaces” (AsiaCCS 2018)** – Uses image analysis on vendors’ product photos to link accounts. The authors train neural embeddings of photos to detect when the same person runs multiple vendor profiles (across markets).  
- **Saxena *et al.*, “IDTraffickers: An Authorship Attribution Dataset…” (EMNLP 2023)** – Releases a large “escort ad” dataset (87k ads, 5k vendors) for stylometric linking. A neural model achieves ~0.865 F1 for vendor identification, illustrating that authorship features in short ads can link rebranded vendor accounts. (While focused on escort ads, it reflects vendor fingerprinting techniques.)  
- **Zhang *et al.*, “Decoding the shadows: multi-modal identity profiling in darknet markets…” (Sensors 2025)** – Proposes a “DarkBIP-FW” framework combining textual (descriptions) and behavioral features (posting times, pricing, categories) with multi-label classifiers to fingerprint vendors. The method significantly outperforms text-only baselines in linking accounts. (Multi-modal stylometry for vendor identity.)  

# Commercial Threat Intelligence Products  
- **Recorded Future:** Offers Dark Web Monitoring as part of its Intelligence Cloud. It collects data from *hundreds* of Tor sites, forums, paste sites, etc., and uses machine learning/NLP to connect dark-web findings with the broader threat landscape. Its Intelligence Graph “correlates signals across technical sources, dark web, open web, and adversary infrastructure”. The platform alerts on compromised credentials, leaks, new exploits and maps them to known threat actors.  
- **DarkOwl:** Claims “the most comprehensive darknet data commercially available.” DarkOwl continuously crawls marketplaces, forums and encrypted sources; its database indexes billions of pages with Tor Onion URLs. Through its Vision UI and APIs, users can search and stream dark-web content, with filters for leaked credentials, fraud/victimology, ransomware chatter, etc. DarkOwl emphasizes automated monitoring and AI-assisted analysis so analysts can prioritize relevant threats.  
- **Flashpoint:** Provides “primary-source” intelligence from closed cybercrime communities. They maintain direct access (including fake personas) into 1,000+ forums, plus “18.4B+ messages” from 200k+ chat channels, and index 847+ transactional markets with OCR on carding data. Flashpoint’s platform offers real-time feeds of new exploit sales, stolen credentials, and ransomware leak posts. Their emphasis is on deep visibility (multilingual human analysts) into cartel-like networks that simple scrapers can’t reach.  
- **Searchlight Cyber:** Their Preemptive Threat Exposure Management (PTEM) platform focuses on “exposures” and attacker intent. It continuously scans an organization’s Internet-facing assets and correlates that with threat actor activity. Specifically it monitors leaked corporate credentials, dark web forum chatter, and exploit discussions. As one summary notes, it provides “law-enforcement-grade intel from cybercriminal forums, closed Telegram channels, & leak sites,” prioritizing only validated high-confidence threats.  
- **ShadowDragon (CACI Horizon):** An enterprise OSINT platform that includes dark-web monitoring. Its DarkBlue Intelligence Suite can index deep/dark web forums and markets and link those data to open-source signals. For example, ShadowDragon’s tools can correlate aliases and transaction data across social media, payment apps, and dark forums. (This is marketed for law enforcement investigations: integrated link analysis of public and dark-web traces.)  

# Public GitHub Repositories (SIH-related)  
- I found **no official GitHub repo** explicitly matching SIH problem 26151. One project *from a security researcher* – **“Xpose”** by prakhar0x01 – advertises itself as “a powerful ... solution for advanced deanonymization” of dark-web sites. It mentions using “OSINT and fingerprinting” to expose hidden services. However, it appears to be a personal research tool (only 1 star, few commits) and not an official solution. Any such repo claiming to solve the full SIH problem should be treated with skepticism. We did not find any completed SIH submissions published. (General OSINT tools like “Robin” exist for dark-web search, but none specifically implement all SIH requirements.)
